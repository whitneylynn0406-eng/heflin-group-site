What people worry about first

What if it gets something wrong?

AI can get things wrong. On Day 2 you build a reviewer, a routine you run: a second AI reads the first one's draft against your own documents and flags what doesn't match. You make the final call.

Will it change or delete my files?

What it can do

These run in the instructor's own control room every week. Each is made the way you'll make yours, from skills and agents. A skill is a saved set of steps your assistant follows. An agent is an assistant that does a job on its own.

Your day, drafted before you're awake.

A daily briefing waiting when you sit down — your calendar, what matters, what to watch. You build this one on Day 2.

Walk into every hard conversation prepared.

Before a meeting that matters, it pulls what you already know about the person and what's at stake, and hands back what to land, what to avoid, and your edge.

Keep what matters when you close the laptop.

At the end of a working session, it scans back for anything worth keeping — a decision, a new contact, a commitment — and asks before it saves. It does the remembering for you.

Three more examples
Call a meeting with your own board.

A standing panel of advisors with different lenses — the builder, the investor, the performance scientist, the meaning-maker — on call anytime a decision is big enough to want a second opinion.

Give it eyes.

Your workout, waiting on your phone.

Today's session arrives with what you did last time and your next target. Text back what you actually did, in shorthand, and it's logged.

The two days

Wednesday, December 9 & Thursday, December 10, 2026. Four two-hour live virtual sessions, 9:30–11:30 a.m. and 1:00–3:00 p.m. ET each day, eight hours of instruction in all. Each module is taught, demonstrated live, then built by you.

Day 1 — Build your chief of staff

  • The reframe. Install Claude Code and get it running on your laptop, then go from chatting with AI to a system that knows your world.
  • Your boot file. The one file your assistant reads at the start of every session. It turns a generic assistant into your chief of staff.
  • Context & memory. Teach it your real world, so it's right between sessions.
  • Where your information goes. See where it goes when you use Claude, and set the privacy choices on your own account.
  • Permissions. AI can give a different answer each time. Here you set rules for what it may and may not do, so you get the results you want and prevent the ones you don't.

Day 2 — Build the team that checks its work

  • Your first skill. A reusable move for what you do every week, saved as a set of steps it follows.
  • Your first agent. A working “Morning Brief,” built live. An agent does a job on its own.
  • Your reviewer. Build a reviewer that can read your draft and flag problems but can't change anything. It ties every number back to its source document, and you watch it catch an error we planted.
  • Staff by cost of being wrong. Choose which AI model does which job (the model is the engine doing the work), and which work gets a second look before it reaches you.
  • Back it up. Set up a backup of what you've built so you can restore it, plus a simple plan for what to build next.

By the end, you have a chief of staff that knows you, a team around it that checks its work, and the templates to keep building. Add the next skill or agent the same way and your setup grows with your work, from a single assistant to an agentic workforce.

Optional lab sessions

Extra hands-on lab time on both days, right before each main session: 9:00–9:30 a.m. and 12:30–1:00 p.m. ET. Completely optional and on top of the eight core hours — drop in for more build time, or skip them entirely.

One thing built the same way: an inventory dashboard for a Louisville fiber-arts retailer

One proof the method works: the skills and agents you'll learn, applied to a store. Yours can be smaller and closer to home.

What it did

A dashboard tested on practice data first.

Before any real data was involved, the team built a working inventory dashboard on made-up orders and products. That practice set stayed on as a test: after any change, the same numbers have to come out.

A dashboard that checks itself against her own store.

Once the store's Shopify exports arrived, the dashboard was rebuilt to read them, and a check compares its totals to the source files. When the tool once recommended reordering gift cards, the fix went into all four places that recommendation is calculated.

Store changes made safely.

A Shopify configuration skill looks at the store the way a visitor sees it, pulls Shopify's current help pages for every setting it touches, and writes the steps with a link at each one. For any change that would make a store visible or buyable, it locks purchasing first and checks as a logged-out visitor before calling the change done.

FAQs

Is this for me? 7 questions
Is this going to be a good fit for me?

It fits you if you work on a computer and you're tired of explaining yourself to an AI from scratch every time. The course is built for beginners: if you can describe what you want in plain English, you can build it. You choose a real task from your own work or life and build around it.

What if I can't use Claude Code at work?

You don't need to. This course is built on and for your own personal laptop and your own work, and there is plenty to build there: a daily briefing waiting when you sit down, preparation before a conversation that matters, and a record of what is worth keeping from each working session. Anything that belongs to your employer, such as their files, their clients' information or their laptop, falls under your employer's rules.

Is it only for certain kinds of jobs?

No. It's for anyone who works on a computer and wants an assistant that already knows their work and their week. You bring a real task of your own and build around it.

I've only used ChatGPT. Does that matter?

No. You don't need any AI experience, and the course starts with the basics. You build in Claude, so you set up a Claude account first, and the setup checklist walks you through it.

Will it work for my kind of work?

You choose the task, so you build it around your own work. The method is the same for any task: name a recurring job, tell the assistant what it can see and what the rules are, and test it.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. You don't write code in this course. You work in a terminal window, and you type plain-English instructions to Claude. If you get stuck on setup, an optional tech-support session before class is open to you, and optional lab time before each session gives you more hands-on time.

Where do I start if I don't know what to build?

Day 1 starts from a blank page: you write the one file your assistant reads at the start of every session. Then you pick one thing you do every week, and on Day 2 you turn it into your first skill, a reusable set of steps. You leave with templates to keep building and a simple plan for what to build next. An optional tech-support session before class gets your laptop ready.

Privacy, safety and accuracy 4 questions
What if the AI gets something wrong?

AI can get things wrong. On Day 2 you build a separate reviewer: a second AI that reads the first one's draft against your own documents and flags what doesn't match. It's a routine you run, and sign-off stays with you.

Where does my information go?

What you type, and what you ask it to read, is sent to Anthropic to produce the answer. You control the setting on your own account that decides whether Anthropic may use your chats and coding sessions to improve future models, and you choose what you give it. On Day 1 Whitney walks you through where your information goes, live, and you set your privacy choices. Then you decide what goes in.

The details are on Anthropic's own pages: Claude Code data usage and model training and your data.

Will it change or delete things on my computer?

It can edit files and run commands on your computer, and you decide how much it does without asking. On Day 1 you set those rules live with the instructor: what it can do on its own and what it has to ask you first. On Day 2 you set up a backup of what you've built so you can restore it.

Anthropic's page: Claude Code security.

Is it safe to give it my files?

Use practice or sample material in the course, not anyone else's private information. Beyond the course, you decide what you give it, and anything that belongs to someone else, such as an employer or a client, follows their rules. Heflin Group can't promise how any tool handles your information; Anthropic's pages describe what it does.
